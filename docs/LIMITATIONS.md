# Known limitations and remaining work

Stated plainly, so the demo is never over-sold.

## Design and realism

- Building **shapes and positions are real** (OpenStreetMap); **facades, roof clutter (water tanks, AC units, dishes), awnings, parked cars and palms are generated**. Facades are procedural (no photo per building), so the city is recognisable by its layout and skyline, not by individual frontages.
- The scooter, bicycle, cars and people are original models generated by `tools/models`. They are stylised-realistic: clean forms with baked shading, not photo-scanned.
- People walk, look round, wave and jump aside through a skeleton fitted to the baked figures at load time (`rig.ts`, bones from `tools/models/rig.py`) with automatic skin weights and a procedural walk cycle — not motion-captured animation. Arms move forward rather than out to the side, because the sleeves were baked joined to the coats; when a startled person throws up their hands a small shard of coat can show at the hip. The two people holding trays (restaurant staff, pizza runner) only look round, they do not walk or wave. Walkers follow the pavements and turn back at walls and dead ends; they cross only at signalled junctions, on streets narrower than 22 m. Up to 9 (medium) or 18 (high) are out at once around the player, none at low quality.
- Street furniture (bins, cones, café tables and chairs, boxes) is knocked about with a simple model (shove, pop, tip onto its side, slide), not a rigid-body solver; things do not stack or hit each other, and they return to their places once the player is 110 m away.
- Traffic drives in lanes, stops at red lights, keeps its distance, turns at junctions and honks at a player who blocks it; it does not change lanes or use roundabout give-way rules (roundabouts are driven as ordinary junctions).
- Vehicle physics is a fast single-body model (grip-limited braking and cornering, lean, suspension, impacts with bounce and scrape), not a tyre or multibody simulation. A crash throws the bike down as a whole; the rider does not ragdoll separately.
- The models add about 7 MB to the first load (cached afterwards). If they fail to load within 25 seconds, the game uses the older procedural models.
- Interiors are a 2D "inside the shop" panel for interior pickups, not a 3D interior scene.
- Day/night and weather are real-time (sun path for Erbil, sky, clouds, rain, fog, wet roads, lamps), but: global illumination is approximated by the sky light probe and ambient light (no real-time GI); reflections come from the sky probe (high quality: on wet roads and glass) — there are no screen-space reflections of buildings or cars; at night only the nearest 3 (medium) or 6 (high) street lamps are real lights, the rest are drawn light pools; tyres throw up spray on a wet road, but there are no splashes from raindrops and no wet windscreens. The sky probe used for reflections is the daytime photo, dimmed at night.
- The rider model was generated with the delivery bag on its back; the game removes the bag's triangles and mounts a separate box on the scooter. A small badge from the old uniform print remains on the rider's chest texture, and the bicycle rider still wears the bag (the bicycle has a front basket).
- Sound is synthesised in the browser (engine, tyres, brakes, impacts, rain, horns), not recorded samples.
- Mirrors are real render-to-texture views but at reduced resolution (96–256 px) and, at low quality, updated every third frame.
- Windows have depth (the side walls of the openings show as the view angle changes) through a parallax effect in the facade shader, not real geometry; it is off at low quality. Shop canopies, roof copings and wall-mounted air conditioners are added only on street-facing buildings and drawn within 170–320 m.
- Small detail is drawn only near the camera (roof tanks and air conditioners about 170 m, parked cars in full detail 150 m then as simple blocks to 420 m, road markings 240 m, trees 380 m, power lines 260–320 m). From far above (the overhead camera) roofs look bare beyond that distance.

## Product scope

- Players are anonymous (browser cookie). There are no player accounts or cross-device progress.
- Only one admin role exists; there are no merchant self-service logins.
- `location_limit` on a package caps concurrent sponsored locations; `featured_limit` caps the featured directory slots. Exclusive geographic ownership is not implemented and is not claimed.
- Analytics history shown on first start is generated demo data (labelled as such). Only events after that are real prototype events.
- Package prices are illustrative. There is no payment, billing, subscription, invoice or renewal anywhere.
